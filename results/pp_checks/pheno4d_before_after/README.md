# Pheno4D soil-removal inspection: why P4 scores below P4a on the held-out target

Generated by `scripts/14_pheno4d_inspection.py` (GPU job 351955, 2026-10-14). Nothing was trained. Inputs:
the P4 and P4a caches in `data/preprocessed_variants/`, the protocol-selected checkpoints
`results/PP_{P4,P4a}_s{1,2,3}/model.pt`, and (for plant points lost to the cut) the earlier raw-cloud
check `results/pp_checks/pheno4d_soil_removal_annotated.csv` (margin 2.0 mm).

Scope note: per-cloud predictions exist only for the **selected checkpoints** (target acc P4 0.730, P4a 0.884,
3-seed means). The full-run means quoted in the plan (0.703 vs 0.863) average over all 100 epochs, and the
per-epoch predictions were not saved, so the breakdown below explains the selected-checkpoint gap. All six
checkpoints reproduce their run.log FINAL lines exactly on GPU (on CPU, P4a s1 is off by one cloud, a known
precision effect: step_notes/PP_2_Results.md).

## Findings, ranked by how strongly the data supports them

**1. The whole P4 deficit is early-season tomato clouds being called maize. (Strong: direct counts, all 3 seeds.)**

| | Tomato recall | Maize recall |
|---|---|---|
| P4a | 0.933 ± 0.076 | 0.797 ± 0.050 |
| P4 | 0.575 ± 0.109 | 1.000 ± 0.000 |

- 15 clouds are wrong in P4 (≥2/3 seeds) but right in P4a (≥2/3 seeds). **All 15 are tomato, scanned
  305–313 (early season, plants 27–64 mm tall in P4)**, from both held-out plants (T01, T03). P4 gives
  most of them maize probability > 0.99.
- Accuracy by growth stage (3-seed mean): P4 on early tomato **0.119** vs P4a **1.000**. On mid/late tomato,
  P4 is 0.786/0.861 vs P4a 0.881/0.917.
- Clouds excluded from the early stage (41 clouds): P4 0.886, P4a 0.935. Early clouds only (22): P4 0.439, P4a 0.788.

**2. P4a's advantage on early clouds is not plant recognition either. (Strong, same data.)** P4a gets 4
early-maize clouds (313–314) wrong on all 3 seeds and calls them tomato, while P4 gets every maize cloud right.
On early clouds both models are close to species-blind, but with opposite biases: P4a calls them tomato and P4
calls them maize. The held-out set has 14 early tomato clouds and 8 early maize, so P4a's tomato bias scores
higher. In the P4a renders, early clouds are almost entirely the square pot surface (90–97% soil-labelled points),
and the plant is a small speck.

**3. After the soil cut, small early plants still carry a large soil remnant. That remnant goes with the
maize calls. (Supported, but confounded with plant size; see 4.)**
- The cut removes 89–99% of soil points (`soil_removed_frac`) on every annotated tomato cloud, as validated
  before. But the plant is tiny early on, so the remaining soil is a large share of the 4096 points: in P4,
  soil-labelled points make up a median of **0.44** of the flipped clouds versus **0.04** in the rest (tomato,
  Mann–Whitney p = 2.5e-5, n = 8 vs 14 annotated). The renders show it as a dense clump around the stem base.
- Across annotated tomato clouds, P4's maize probability rises with the leftover-soil fraction (Spearman
  ρ = 0.79, p = 1.5e-5).
- The early maize clouds have the same structure in P4 (upright stem plus a soil clump; e.g. M04_0313_a: 51%
  soil) and P4 calls them maize. Reading (a guess, not tested): in P4, "small upright plant on a soil clump" is
  treated as maize whatever the species.
- The soil labels use the inferred mapping (raw id 0 = soil, `adapters/dataset.py::pheno4d_collapse_organ_labels`)
  and exist only for the 36 annotated held-out clouds.

**4. Plant size / growth stage is collinear with finding 3 and cannot be separated here. (Measured, not
resolved.)** Among tomato clouds, the flipped ones are smaller (P4 height median 42 vs 87 mm, Spearman height
vs P4 maize probability ρ = −0.70) and earlier (median date 308 vs 318). Small plant and large soil share go
together, so these numbers cannot say whether the soil remnant, the small plant, or both drive the maize calls.
A weak hint against "size alone": at the same dates T01 (smaller) is right more often than T03 (e.g. 310: T01
34 mm 2/3 right, T03 54 mm 0/3 right). That is only a few clouds, so it is not evidence.

**5. Normalised point spacing for Pheno4D tomato moves away from Crops3D tomato in P4. (Measured; most likely a
side effect of 3, not a separate cause.)** The mean nearest-neighbour distance in the normalised space (what the
network sees) of Pheno4D tomato against Crops3D tomato: KS 0.33 in P4a and 0.80 in P4. It moves towards Crops3D
maize (KS 0.995 → 0.73). Within tomato, the flipped clouds are denser (0.0117 vs 0.0148, p = 1e-4). A dense soil
clump lowers the mean spacing, so this is plausibly the same thing as finding 3.

## Hypotheses the data does not support

- **Truncated stems.** Plant points lost to the cut are ~0 on the flipped clouds (median 0.05% of plant
  points, max 0.7%; raw-cloud check, annotated clouds only). The renders show intact stems down to the soil
  remnant.
- **Leftover pot structure.** No rim or pot wall is visible in any P4 render. The remnant is soil around the
  stem base, not pot. This matches the earlier pot-rim check (step_notes/PP_0, no separate rim in Pheno4D).
- **Different overall size/shape after rescaling.** At dataset level, the soil cut moves Pheno4D tomato
  *closer* to Crops3D tomato on every shape statistic in normalised space (KS vs Crops3D tomato, P4a → P4:
  height 0.92 → 0.74, width 0.71 → 0.31, height/width 0.85 → 0.66, bottom z 0.92 → 0.74, mean radius 0.71 →
  0.35). Maize height also gets closer (0.66 → 0.10). Removing soil makes the target look more like the source
  on shape, so a general size/shape mismatch does not explain the drop.
- **Absolute size and density differences between the datasets** are the largest of all (KS 0.9–1.0: scale,
  height in mm, spacing in mm, raw point count; Pheno4D plants are 3–6× smaller and ~4× more finely sampled).
  But they are equally large in P4 and P4a, and normalisation removes them before the network, so they cannot
  explain a P4-vs-P4a difference.

## Not computed

- Per-cloud predictions over all 100 epochs (only the selected checkpoint is saved), so the full-run-mean gap
  is not broken down, only the selected-checkpoint gap.
- Point counts before FPS are taken from the manifest (`n_after_soil_removal`, `n_predecimated`); they were not
  recomputed from raw clouds (not on the cluster).
- Soil fraction and plant loss for the 27 unannotated held-out clouds: there are no labels.
- Plant age: only the scan date is available (read as March days, 305 = 5 March; an inference from the file names).
  Stages are three equal groups of the distinct dates per species.
